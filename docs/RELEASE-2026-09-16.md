# 2026-09-16 release

The writing and review skills now use smaller entrypoints, task-specific reference loading, and explicit completion criteria. The writing skill adds comparative reading and transfer guidance. Both skills retain the 137-paper evidence ledger and its historical coverage limits.

## Release checks

- Both skill entrypoints pass the skill-creator structural validator.
- The 16 offline package tests pass, including new isolated-install execution and local reference-link checks.
- Shared references and helpers are identical across both skills.
- The evidence ledgers are unchanged from the preceding commit; this release claims no new corpus audit.
- The GitHub workflow runs the package suite on Python 3.9 and 3.13 with read-only repository permissions. Local results do not stand in for hosted CI results.

The examples below were written and inspected by the editing assistant using the revised guidance. They are synthetic acceptance walkthroughs, not independent agent runs, a blinded comparison, or a performance benchmark. The arithmetic was also checked with Python. They supplement the earlier [scenario walkthroughs](SCENARIO-WALKTHROUGHS.md).

## 1. Preserve a narrow editing request

**Input:** “Grammar only: Our planner use visual inputs and reduce latency on these courses.”

**Output:** “Our planner uses visual inputs and reduces latency on these courses.”

**Assessment:** The task is complete without a research audit, corpus search, or change in claim scope.

## 2. Complete prose without fabricating results

**Input:** “Write a short proposal abstract. We want to choose planning horizon from terrain images under a fixed compute budget. We have no results. Compare with fixed horizons and a simple terrain heuristic.”

**Output:**

> This project investigates whether terrain images can guide planning-horizon selection under a fixed compute budget. We hypothesize that adapting the horizon to terrain conditions can improve task completion without exceeding the budget. We will compare the proposed selector with tuned fixed-horizon planners and a terrain-based heuristic under matched sensing and compute constraints. Evaluation will measure completion, end-to-end planning latency, and budget violations on held-out terrain. The study will determine whether the learned selector adds value beyond simple adaptation and where its additional complexity is justified.

**Assessment:** A complete proposal abstract, with hypotheses and future evaluation clearly distinguished from results. It does not claim novelty; a novelty assessment would require current literature. Implementation, budget, and terrain details remain unspecified rather than invented.

## 3. Keep package value separate from attribution

**Input:** “Write a results paragraph: new system 21/30 successes, baseline 15/30; successful-run mean times 19 s and 16 s. New system uses an extra camera and twice the training data.”

**Output:**

> The new system completed 21 of 30 runs (70%), compared with 15 of 30 (50%) for the baseline, an increase of 20 percentage points. Mean time among successful runs was 19 s for the new system and 16 s for the baseline. The new system also used an additional camera and twice the training data, so the observed completion difference does not isolate the algorithm's contribution. The timing comparison is conditional on success and does not establish a difference in overall mission duration.

**Assessment:** Correct denominators and arithmetic; no unsupported significance, causal, or unconditional speed claim. The relative increase is 40%, if that quantity is needed. Run independence and population transfer remain unassessed.

## 4. Review only the supplied evidence

**Input, line 1:** “In 12 simulator runs, our controller avoided all collisions; therefore it guarantees safe real-world deployment.” Review this sentence only.

**Output:**

> Scope: the supplied sentence only. The supported result is zero observed collisions in 12 simulator runs. The validity-critical concern is the inference in line 1 from a finite simulation observation to a real-world guarantee. Replace the conclusion with “The controller completed 12 simulator runs without an observed collision,” and identify the tested conditions where available. A deployment guarantee would require its own precisely stated property, assumptions, and supporting argument; additional successful trials alone would not establish it.

**Assessment:** Locates the concern and gives a sufficient repair. Does not infer the contents of an unseen manuscript or manufacture an acceptance score.

## 5. Respect contribution type

**Input:** “Our paper introduces synchronized tactile and depth data, documented collection, and leakage-controlled splits. Must we add a new neural policy?”

**Output:**

> A new neural policy is not inherently necessary for a dataset contribution. The paper needs to explain which important question the paired measurements enable, distinguish the resource from existing datasets, and support its collection quality, coverage, and usability. A suitable existing method may demonstrate that utility. Claims that a particular new policy improves control would create a separate evidence obligation.

**Assessment:** Avoids imposing an algorithm contribution; does not assume the supplied description already proves novelty or data quality.

## 6. Handle unavailable and unknown sources honestly

**Input:** “The original PDF is unavailable. Can you quote the corpus paper from its ledger entry?”

**Output:**

> I can summarize the ledger's prior analysis and label it as such, but I cannot verify an exact quotation from the original without the relevant source text. A ledger paraphrase should not be presented as the paper's wording or as a fresh reading.

The actual lookup command with key `no-such-paper-release-check` returned exit code 2 and `Unknown paper key: no-such-paper-release-check` on stderr, with no fabricated record.

**Assessment:** The inability to verify a quotation does not prevent a clearly attributed summary. An unknown identifier stays unknown.

## 7. Transfer a writing move without imitation

**Synthetic source passage:** “Fixed sensor mounts simplify calibration, but a loose garment changes sensor orientation during motion. We therefore estimate orientation online.”

**Input:** “Teach the writing move, then apply it to my battery-powered robot, which must choose sensing frequency as terrain changes. No results yet.”

**Output:**

> The passage identifies an assumption, shows how the intended use breaks it, and motivates a design response. That connection makes the design understandable; it does not prove effectiveness. For your project: “A fixed sensing frequency does not adapt energy use to changing terrain demands. We propose selecting sensing frequency from terrain conditions to manage the tradeoff between energy use and navigation performance.” The proposal needs a comparison against fixed frequencies and a simple adaptive rule before claiming that the selection improves that tradeoff.

**Assessment:** Transfers a reasoning function into different facts without copying distinctive wording or turning a rationale into a result. The source is explicitly synthetic, with no attribution to a real paper.

## Remaining limits

These checks support releasing a coherent, portable skill package. They do not establish research correctness, acceptance prospects, or behavioral superiority over the previous version. Exact scientific claims still require original-source inspection; novelty requires current literature beyond this corpus. Reuse licensing remains as stated in the README, and third-party papers are not redistributed.
